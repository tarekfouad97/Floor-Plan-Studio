Five steps, about ten minutes

You don't need to know CAD. If you can drag things around, you can do this.

1

Bring a photo

Photograph or scan any floor plan and drop it in. It sits behind your drawing as a faint guide. Or skip it and draw from your own measurements.

2

Set the scale

Click two points you know the distance between, type that distance, and everything afterwards is true to size.

3

Trace the walls

Click corner to corner. Type an exact length whenever you want one — 360 means 360 cm, not "roughly".

4

Make the rooms

Click inside any enclosed space and it works out the shape, the size and the floor area for you.

5

Furnish it

Drag from 113 pieces at real dimensions. It warns you when something won't fit, blocks a door, or leaves too little room to walk.

What you get

Built around one idea: the numbers have to be right, or none of it helps.

📐Everything in centimetres

Every wall, every gap, every piece of furniture. Sizes are shown on the drawing and on the printout, so you can check before you buy.

⚠️It tells you what won't work

Furniture through a wall, a wardrobe blocking a door, under 75 cm to squeeze past the sofa — flagged as you go.

🛒A shopping list that builds itself

Item, room, size, quantity, your notes and prices. Print it and take it to the shop.

🧊See it in 3D

Walls stand up, doors and windows cut through, furniture takes shape, and the sun casts real shadows at any hour of the day.

💡Sockets and lighting

Place switches, sockets and lights at proper heights, wire switches to the lights they control, and print a schedule for the electrician.

🔒Yours, and only yours

Nothing is uploaded. Plans are saved in your browser or to a file on your computer. No account, no cloud.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Open it and use it. There's no account and no trial.

Where do my plans go?

Into your own browser's storage, and into files you save yourself. Nothing is sent to a server — the page has no way to upload anything.

Can I use it on my phone?

You can view and adjust a plan, and pinch to zoom in 3D. Drawing a flat from scratch is much easier on a laptop.

How accurate is it?

As accurate as the numbers you give it. Calibrate the photo carefully, or type real tape-measure lengths, and it's exact. A photo of a sketch is only ever as good as the sketch — always measure before buying something expensive.

Can I print it?

Yes — the plan on one page, your shopping list on the next, and a room schedule after that. Or export a PNG to send to a carpenter.
