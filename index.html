How you use it02

Four stages, one afternoon

Nothing here needs a course. You start with whatever you already have — a photo of the estate agent’s plan, or just the room and a tape measure.

STAGE 01

Draw the flat

Trace over a photo of an existing plan, or draw the walls yourself. Every length you type is real centimetres.

  • Trace a photo — drop in a scan or a phone photo, set the scale from one known distance, and let it find the walls
  • Snap to 1, 5, 10 or 25 cm, or turn it off
  • Doors, windows and openings that cut the wall properly
  • Several floors in one file, each with its own plan
STAGE 02

Put things in it

A catalogue of 113 pieces at industry-standard sizes — sofas, beds, wardrobes, appliances, the lot. Drag, drop, rotate, or type an exact size.

  • Real dimensions on every piece, always in centimetres
  • Moods — lighting scenes you can switch between
  • Ask Claude — copy the room out, paste up to five furnished layouts back in, and flick between them on the plan
STAGE 03

See what breaks

The plan argues back. Clearances, door swings and blocked routes are flagged while you work, not after you’ve bought the wardrobe.

  • Checks for walkways, pull-out space and doors that hit furniture
  • Room areas in square metres, kept up to date
  • 3D view with cutaway walls, eye level, and the sun placed by time of day and which way the flat faces
  • Sockets, switches and circuits planned on the same drawing
STAGE 04

Take it out of the screen

The point of the whole thing: paper you can carry into a shop, and a file you can come back to next weekend.

  • Print or PDF — a plan page per floor on A4, plus schedules
  • An illustrated shopping list with every size in centimetres
  • CSV and PNG when you need the numbers or the picture elsewhere
  • A .json file you save yourself and reopen any time
What you walk away with03

A shopping list with the sizes already on it

Most planners give you a pretty picture. This one gives you the document you actually need in the shop: every piece, grouped by room, with the width and depth you measured for, so you can check the label against your own plan before it goes in the van.

How it is built04

It never sends your flat anywhere

Not as a policy — as a fact about how it is written. The planner is one HTML file that runs in your browser. There is no server for it to upload to.

No account

No sign-up, no email, no password. Open the page and start drawing. There is nothing for anyone to sign in to.

Your file is yours

Work in progress is kept in your own browser’s storage. Save to a .json file on your computer and it is yours to keep, move, back up or delete.

Works offline

Once the page has loaded, pull the plug on the wi-fi and carry on. Nothing on this site loads from anywhere else — no fonts, no analytics, no trackers.

The full detail is in the privacy policy.

Questions05
Is it really free?
Yes. There is no paid tier, no trial that expires and no feature held back. It is a single web page, so it costs almost nothing to run.
Do I need to install anything or make an account?
Neither. Open the planner in any modern browser and it is ready. Nothing is downloaded to your machine except the files you choose to save.
What units does it use?
Centimetres, everywhere, deliberately. Mixing metres and millimetres is how people end up ordering a worktop 10 cm short. Areas are shown in square metres.
I have a PDF or a photo of my flat’s plan. Can I use it?
Yes — that is the fastest way to start. Load the image as a trace underlay, tell the planner the real distance between two points on it so it knows the scale, then either draw over it or let it find the walls for you and tidy up the corners.
Does it work on a phone or tablet?
It runs, and the plan pinches and pans, but drawing is fiddly on a small screen. A laptop or a tablet with a keyboard is a much better time. The printed output is the same either way.
Where does the furniture catalogue come from?
The 113 pieces use generic industry-standard dimensions, not any one manufacturer’s data. They are there so you can plan with something realistic before you have chosen a specific product — measure the real thing before you buy it.
How does the “Furnish with Claude” part work?
The planner writes out a description of your rooms and the catalogue and puts it on your clipboard. You paste it into Claude, in your own account, and paste the answer back. The planner itself never talks to Claude or to any other service — it has no network code in it at all.
Can I use it for a house, an office or a garden?
Yes. It handles several floors, indoor and outdoor rooms, and wet rooms. It is built around flats because that is what it was written for, but nothing stops you drawing anything that is made of straight walls.
Is this a substitute for an architect or an electrician?
No. It is a planning tool for deciding what to buy and where it goes. The electrical layer helps you brief a qualified electrician and mark up where you want things — it is not a certified design and it is not compliance advice.
