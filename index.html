How it works

Draw it, furnish it, light it

You don't need to know CAD. Every length you type is real centimetres, and everything after that is built on those numbers.

1

Draw

Trace a photo of an existing plan, or click corner to corner and type exact lengths. 360 means 360 cm, not "roughly". Click inside a space and it works out the room and its floor area.

2

Furnish

Drag from 113 pieces at industry-standard dimensions, or hand the whole room to Claude. Every piece shows its real width and depth on the drawing and on the printout.

3

Light

Place fittings at real heights with a colour temperature, wire them into circuits with their switches, then save moods — Evening, Cooking — and switch the whole flat between them.

✦ New

Let Claude furnish and light it

Once your walls are up, the planner writes a description of your flat — every room at its real size, every door and window, and the whole catalogue it is allowed to use. Paste that into Claude, paste the answer back, and it lands on your plan at true dimensions.

It plans the lighting too: which fittings, where, at what colour temperature, wired into circuits with their switches — then writes you moods to switch between. It cannot invent a sofa that does not exist, and anything it gets wrong is reported rather than placed.

The part other planners skip

It tells you what won't work

Anyone can drag a sofa onto a picture. This checks the sofa against the room it is actually in, in centimetres, and says so while you are still deciding.

  • Walkways. Under 75 cm to squeeze past and it says so, with the real gap.
  • Door swings. A wardrobe in the arc of a door is flagged, not drawn over.
  • Through walls. Measured to the wall itself, so a diagonal wall doesn't cry wolf.
  • Circuits. A light on no circuit, or a circuit with no switch, is caught before the electrician is.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Open it and use it. There's no account and no trial.

Do I need a Claude account to use it?

No. Drawing and furnishing by hand needs nothing at all. The Claude feature writes you a prompt to paste in wherever you use Claude, and reads the answer back — the planner itself never talks to a server.

Where do my plans go?

Into your own browser's storage, and into files you save yourself. Nothing is sent anywhere — the page has no way to upload anything.

How accurate is it?

As accurate as the numbers you give it. Calibrate a photo carefully, or type real tape-measure lengths, and it's exact. Always measure before buying something expensive.

Can I use it on my phone?

You can view and adjust a plan, and pinch to zoom in 3D. Drawing a flat from scratch is much easier on a laptop.

Can I print it?

Yes — the plan on one page, your shopping list on the next, then a room schedule and an electrical schedule. Or export a PNG.
